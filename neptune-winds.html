Why does Neptune have such fierce winds?

The coldest-looking worlds can have astonishingly energetic weather.

AI-generated practice research pack prepared for personal study and review. Not commissioned work or evidence of independent authorship.

Opening question / story hook

Neptune is so distant that sunlight takes about four hours to reach it. Yet its atmosphere can move at speeds that would be astonishing even for a fast aircraft. Where does that energy come from?

Evidence and source register

The observation

NASA describes winds exceeding 1,200 mph, approximately 2,000 km/h, on Neptune. These are atmospheric wind estimates, not the planet's orbital or rotational speed.

NASA: Neptune facts ↗

The spacecraft evidence

Voyager 2 encountered Neptune in August 1989 and observed dark storms and fast-moving bright clouds. It remains the only spacecraft to have visited Neptune up close.

NASA: Voyager fact sheet ↗

The energy puzzle

Neptune has a strong internal heat source despite weak incoming sunlight. Energy escaping from its interior is an important contributor to its atmospheric activity.

NASA: ice giant atmospheric research ↗

What remains uncertain

NASA's Hubble discussion connects the contrast between warm internal regions and cold upper clouds with possible atmospheric instabilities. This is a physical explanation to investigate, not a complete quantitative model of the winds.

Research approach

Use the observed wind speed as the starting puzzle. Compare NASA's mission observations with its planetary facts and atmospheric research descriptions, separating observations from explanatory hypotheses.

Accuracy notes

Avoid treating Neptune as a solid icy surface, stating every cloud moves at maximum speed, or claiming the origin of every wind feature is completely solved. No original spacecraft image measurements are presented.
