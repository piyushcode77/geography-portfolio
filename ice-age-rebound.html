Why is Canada still rising after the Ice Age?

A landscape can remember a vanished ice sheet.

AI-generated practice research pack prepared for personal study and review. Not commissioned work or evidence of independent authorship.

Opening question / story hook

Imagine returning to the same spot beside Hudson Bay every year and finding that the ground itself has moved upward. The force responsible is no longer there: the ice sheet disappeared long ago.

Evidence and source register

The mechanism

The weight of continental ice depressed the solid Earth. After the ice retreated, the crust and underlying mantle began a prolonged adjustment. This is glacial isostatic adjustment (GIA).

NOAA: glacial isostatic adjustment ↗

The measurement

A 2006 study using 360 GPS stations in Canada and the United States reported vertical uplift around 10 mm per year near Hudson Bay. This is a regional finding, not a rate for all of Canada.

Sella et al. (2006), Geophysical Research Letters ↗

The satellite connection

GRACE gravity observations provide a different view of the changing distribution of mass. Researchers used these data to constrain the shape of the former Laurentide ice complex.

NASA technical report: GRACE and Laurentia ↗

Why it matters

Relative sea level depends on both the water surface and the moving land. Accounting for rebound helps scientists separate present ice loss from older changes in Earth's shape.

NASA: gravimetry and sea level ↗

Suggested video sequence

  1. Open with a ruler and a 10 mm gap: a year's illustrative uplift near Hudson Bay.
  2. Animate a cross section: ice loading, displaced mantle, ice retreat and delayed recovery. Label it conceptual, not to scale.
  3. Show a GPS station and twin gravity satellites to distinguish position measurements from gravity measurements.
  4. End with a tide gauge: is the water moving, the land moving, or both?

Research approach

Start with the observed motion, then establish the physical mechanism using NOAA, check the numerical claim against a peer-reviewed GPS study, and cross-check the satellite interpretation with NASA.

Accuracy notes

Do not describe the mantle as a liquid ocean or claim the uplift rate is uniform or constant indefinitely. The GPS number comes from a specific published study; no fresh GPS data were analyzed here.
